The Data Centre Impact Model

A proposed data centre can change a place for decades. DCIM helps residents and councils see how: what kinds of impact a site has, which choices make those impacts bigger or smaller, and what to ask for in return.

What the model is

Nine variables, four types of impact

The model describes every data centre with nine variables, the choices and facts you can find in a planning application. Those variables drive four types of impact on the community around the site. Hover over or tap a variable or an impact to trace the links.

How the Data Centre Impact Model fits together

Size is the only variable that touches all four. Grid region changes the environmental impact more than anything else; cooling changes how much water and electricity a site takes.

💷 Economic

Who gains money and work, and who pays.

Jobs, community funds, business rates, electricity bills, house prices.

⚡ Resource extraction

What the site takes from the area.

Electricity, water, land, grid capacity.

🌿 Environmental

What it puts into the air, land and climate.

Carbon, generator noise and fumes, local heat, wildlife.

🏛️ Cultural

How it changes the look, history and feel of a place.

Landscape, green belt, heritage, local customs.

Terms you'll learn

Speak the language of a planning application

Understand explains these as you go. Here they are in one place.

MW (megawatt)How much power a site can draw. It's how data centres are sized; the average UK project is about 100 MW.
GWh (gigawatt-hour)An amount of electricity used over time. 5 GWh would power about 1,850 UK homes for a year.
IT loadThe power used by the servers alone, before cooling and other overheads.
PUEPower usage effectiveness: total electricity divided by server electricity. 1.0 would be perfect; 1.3–1.6 is typical.
Load factorThe share of its capacity a site actually uses over a year, usually 40–80%.
Grid carbon intensityGrams of CO₂ per kWh of electricity in a region. In Scotland it's about 5 g; in South Wales about 260 g.
Carbon valueThe price the government puts on a tonne of CO₂ to weigh costs to society: £264 in its central case.
Local Benefit PlanWhat the UK Data Centre Covenant asks large sites to agree with their community. There's no set amount.
